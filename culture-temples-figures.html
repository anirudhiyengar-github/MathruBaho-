07 — Sacred Ground & Living Legacy

The temples that shaped the tradition, the people who carried it forward

Every rite and recipe on this site traces back to a handful of temple towns — and forward, through centuries, to real people who carried the Iyengar name into science, statecraft, the arts, and yoga studios worldwide.

Srirangam — Where Ramanuja Lived

The Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple at Srirangam, Tiruchirappalli, is the single most important site in Sri Vaishnava geography — often called the "Eastern Fortress" of the tradition. This is where Ramanujacharya spent much of his life, reformed the temple's administration and rituals, and wrote many of his most important philosophical works. His preserved form is still kept within the temple complex today, said to have been maintained with sandalwood paste and saffron for over eight centuries.

Sriperumbudur — The Birthplace

Near present-day Chennai, the town of Sriperumbudur is revered as Ramanuja's birthplace. A temple here honors him directly — local tradition holds that the consecrated image inside was sanctified by Ramanuja himself, and the site remains a pilgrimage destination for Sri Vaishnavas tracing the philosopher's life story from birth to his final years at Srirangam.

Melkote — The Western Fortress

Known to Sri Vaishnavas as Thirunarayanapuram, Melkote in Karnataka is considered the "Western Fortress" counterpart to Srirangam. Ramanuja lived here for roughly a dozen years after leaving Tamil Nadu, establishing the worship of Cheluva Narayana with the support of the local Hoysala king. Melkote is also known for the Araiyar Sevai, a ritual song-and-dance enactment of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham — one of only a handful of temples where this tradition still survives.

"Srirangam, Melkote, Kanchipuram, and Tirumala — the four Swayambu Kshetrams that anchor Sri Vaishnava sacred geography."

Kanchipuram & Tirumala

Two more sites complete this sacred geography. The Varadaraja Perumal Temple in Kanchipuram is where a young Ramanuja studied under his early teacher before charting his own philosophical path. And Tirumala, home to the temple of Venkateswara, is among the most visited pilgrimage sites in the world — a devotional center whose reach extends well beyond the Iyengar community, but which remains deeply woven into Sri Vaishnava practice.

Early & Modern Scholars

The philosophical lineage traced earlier on this site has real names attached to it. Vedanta Desika (1268–1369 CE), whose teachings anchor the Vadakalai school, was a prolific philosopher, poet, and one of the most influential commentators the tradition has produced. Centuries later, that same scholarly instinct shows up in figures like M. Raghava Iyengar (1878–1960), a noted Tamil literary scholar whose research helped establish the historical timelines of classical Tamil poets and the Alvars, and Thiru Narayana Iyengar (1861–1947), a Tamil and Sanskrit scholar central to the founding of the Madurai Tamil Sangam.

Statecraft, Science & Global Reach

The community's reach extends well beyond religious scholarship. C. Rajagopalachari (1878–1972) — statesman, writer, and independence activist — served as the last Governor-General of India and remains one of the most significant political figures of the 20th century. And in a very different arena, B.K.S. Iyengar (1918–2014) took a practice with roots in the same broader tradition and introduced it to a global audience: the style of yoga bearing his name, built around precision and alignment, is now taught in schools across more than 250 cities worldwide.
